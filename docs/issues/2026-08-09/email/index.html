Intrinsic Motivation Makes All The Difference

  • Teaching in the Age of AI

“Human beings are naturally curious creatures - we want to learn new skills and new things all the time. Throughout the bulk of human history, the motivation was not external validation in the form of grades. It was something deeper, more primal, and fundamental, often life-saving. My teaching log for my graduate program in the late 90’s included this provocative question: Think back to a time when you had a high level of success in learning something that you feel was challenging to you. What was the nature of the learning situation that enabled you to master these difficult ideas or skills?”

A.I. Update

Lesson design to avoid cognitive offloading, 15 ways to use Claude, existential risk, and more

Two excellent features this week. First, Dr. Philippa Hardman’s post on designing around cognitive offloading is the best piece yet I’ve seen on crafting learning experiences to use AI effectively for learning. It should be a reference source for anyone working in your school community to explore AI-aware lesson development.

The second feature is similarly practical: a teacher who uses Claude regularly describes the 15 ways he uses Claude. If you’re interested in exploring how AI might support your workflow, this one is practical and quick.

Also this week, a slate of concerns about AI: researchers develop viable viruses with AI (not ones that can harm humans, though), AI companions are found to worsen loneliness for some people, and more.

At the same time, the question of the value of AI-generated content (or content generated with the assistance of AI) is only growing more complex. What does it mean if we find a story to be meaningful to us — until we found out it was written by AI? Should the source of a work of art determine its value to us? Or is the feeling or thought it summons what is most important? We will inevitably be debating this for years. This week adds a few reflections to that debate.

All these and more, enjoy!

Best Practical Advice Yet: “How To Design Around Cognitive Offloading”

  • Dr Philippa Hardman

“For two years, the cognitive offloading debate has been quite binary and vague on specifics: we need to ban AI from the learning experience because it erodes cognition, or let’s embrace AI because it helps us get smarter. A number of studies published in 2026 have helped to move the debate beyond the binary and replace it with a more nuanced (but still incomplete) understanding of conditions under which offloading harms learning, the conditions under which AI improves cognition, and how to design for the difference.”

Scientists Just Made Lab Viruses With AI. What Could Go Wrong?

  • New York Times

“Scientists at Stanford University and the Arc Institute, a research organization in Palo Alto, Calif., taught A.I. to recognize patterns of DNA structure in nature, and then to use that data to write recipes for entirely new viruses. The researchers followed those recipes to create DNA molecules, which they inserted into bacteria. The modified bacteria then produced viruses never seen in nature. The viruses were able to infect other bacteria, demonstrating that they were viable.”
