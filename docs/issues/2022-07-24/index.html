Educator’s Notebook #387 (July 24, 2022)

Two excellent feature articles, and many more this week.

To help ground your campus conversations about equity, be sure everyone is on the same page about what equity means. See the featured piece by the Center for Curriculum Redesign for a primer on different perspectives about equity.

In the second featured piece, The Verge offers a nuanced (and creatively presented) exploration into the writers who are using A.I. to help them write both fiction and nonfiction. Reading this piece, I'm reminded of the recognition in the chess world that in a competition between the most intelligent humans, the most powerful computers, and pretty good humans with pretty good computers, the last category wins. Pretty good people with pretty good computers can do amazing things in chess. The question is: (how) does this carry into something like writing, especially writing that aims to explore the human experience? This is a topic for rich discussion.

Also this week, find several pieces on the Webb Telescope, more tools for turning debate into discussion, and a nice closer by David Brooks about how perceiving life as a game or a journey changes how we engage it.

These and more, enjoy!

Meet The Writers Using A.I. To Help Them Write Their Novels

The Verge · 07/20/22

“She’s a little embarrassed to say she’s become reliant on it. Not that she couldn’t write without it, but she thinks her writing wouldn’t be as rich, and she would certainly be more burnt out. “There’s something different about working with the AI and editing those words, and then coming up with my own and then editing it, that’s much easier. It’s less emotionally taxing. It’s less tiresome; it’s less fatiguing. I need to pay attention much less closely. I don’t get as deeply into the writing as I did before, and yet, I found a balance where I still feel very connected to the story, and I still feel it’s wholly mine.””
