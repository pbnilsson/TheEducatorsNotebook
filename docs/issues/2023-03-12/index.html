Introduction

A reflective week.

One of this week's feature posts tells us the story we don't typically hear about the history of science: not the one about the Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment, but instead one about global conversation over centuries that led to the innovation in knowledge and technology ultimately centered in some geographies. I'm looking forward to the books it reviews.

The writing on generative A.I. is growing more reflective -- and imaginative. While some may tire of the subject, it's an important one to stay attentive to. The personal computer, mobile device, and internet have changed our classrooms and experiences of learning (for better, for worse), and this will no doubt do the same, but faster and more significantly than the others. Still, while individual technologies may scale and change us more and more quickly, societal change might actually be slowing down. See the tech section(s) for more.

Also, this week is the final issue to include the now-complete series of Exercises for the Recovery of Childhood. I hope this set of poems written by my former colleague Andy Stallings brought you some joy during the pandemic. Visit the website to explore all 62 of them in an interactive space. The Instagram and Facebook pages are far behind and will be updated in the future, so if you want to keep these drops of lightheartedness in your life, consider following them there -- or sharing your email address at the website for a print edition that may come out in the future.

These and many more articles this week,

Enjoy!

Over the past 20 years, sponsors pointed to two key strengths — an ability to drive results and functional or technical expertise — as the central reasons for identifying and nominating employees as high potentials. But to reach the next level, high potentials who’ve been rewarded for personal accomplishments must learn to recalibrate to a definition of success based on the team’s collective performance. Relying on a past track record of success will not be enough as high potentials grapple with the scale, scope, and complexity of more senior general management roles.”
