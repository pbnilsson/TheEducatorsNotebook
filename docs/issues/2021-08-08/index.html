Introduction

A curious week this week...

Curiosity may be the most significant source of intrinsically motivated learning, which may be the source of the strongest lifelong learning. This week, find several articles on the topic of curiosity, and if this is of interest to you, consider searching the archive of Educator's Notebook articles on the topic of curiosity.

Otherwise, this week's feature pieces explore what makes for great listening (which includes a healthy amount of active learning) and a nice compilation of the kinds of questions we can ask ourselves as we begin a new school year.

Find these and more in this week's collection.

Enjoy!

Curiosity And The Absence Of Certainty, Awareness Of Ignorance

Austin Kleon · 08/03/21

“I believe that one of the greatest mistakes made by human beings is to want certainties when trying to understand something. The search for knowledge is not nourished by certainty: it is nourished by a radical absence of certainty. Thanks to the acute awareness of our ignorance, we are open to doubt and can continue to learn and to learn better. This has always been the strength of scientific thinking—thinking born of curiosity, revolt, change.”
