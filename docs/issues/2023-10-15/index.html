Introduction

Several key notes to kick off this issue:

• A Musical

• Israel and Gaza

• Articles

• A.I.

Musical: Several years ago, I wrote a short musical about schools. For the past two weeks/issues, I was in New York City putting the musical up at the New York Times Center during EdTechWeek. (Thank you for your patience with the absence of newsletters during this time -- long issue this week!). Most of all: what a thrill it was to work with Broadway performers from Hamilton, Moulin Rouge, Six, Aladdin, and a host of other shows for this one-time performance focused on education. I'll share photos and video when they are prepared.

In the meantime, these weeks have been full of education news, and of course global news from the Middle East.

Israel and Gaza: Are you struggling with how to navigate the current situation in Israel and Gaza? See the Features and Humanities section for guidance on how to engage this complex topic with students. In addition to several other resources, also find a link to the Wikipedia article on the Israel-Hamas war. Though it has its shortcomings, Wikipedia continues to one of the most up to date and most factual resources for current events unfolding globally.

Articles: In teaching and learning, find useful pieces on assessment formats, on mobile devices, on group work and more. Also, whether or not you were able to engage the solar eclipse yesterday, don't miss the total eclipse happening this spring. See the STEM section for when it will be and start making your plans.

Of particular interest to me is creator of Calvin & Hobbes Bill Watterson's release of his first book in almost 30 years. In Creativity, find a fascinating video detailing his collaboration with a visual artist to bring it to life. More on the book itself in the Other section.

A.I.: Last, see the A.I. section at the end for another round of extraordinary developments. New technologies continue to emerge at a breakneck pace, and we are entering a new phase of generative A.I. development as schools and industry begin diving more deeply into the practical application of A.I. to their domains.

These and more,

Enjoy!

Peter

Are We Living In The Least Artistic Century In 500 Years?

New York Times · 10/10/23

“More than the economics, the key factor can only be what happened to us at the start of this century: first, the plunge through our screens into an infinity of information; soon after, our submission to algorithmic recommendation engines and the surveillance that powers them. The digital tools we embraced were heralded as catalysts of cultural progress, but they produced such chronological confusion that progress itself made no sense. “It’s still one Earth,” the novelist Stacey D’Erasmo wrote in 2014, “but it is now subtended by a layer of highly elastic non-time, wild time, that is akin to a global collective unconscious wherein past, present and future occupy one unmediated plane.” In this dark wood, today and yesterday become hard to distinguish. The years are only time stamps. Objects lose their dimensions. Everything is recorded, nothing is remembered; culture is a thing to nibble at, to graze on.”

“Reskilling In The Age Of A.I.”

Harvard Business Review · 10/01/23

“The authors… interviewed leaders at some 40 organizations around the world that are investing in large-scale reskilling programs. In synthesizing what they learned, they became aware of five paradigm shifts that are emerging in reskilling: (1) Reskilling is a strategic imperative. (2) It is the responsibility of every leader and manager. (3) It is a change-management initiative. (4) Employees want to reskill—when it makes sense. (5) It takes a village. The authors argue that companies will need to understand and embrace these shifts if they hope to succeed in adapting dynamically to the rapidly evolving new era of automation and AI.”
