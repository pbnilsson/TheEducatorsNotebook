Introduction

A week of thoughtfulness and joy.

The end of year tends to fill with "year in review" listicles that I confess I enjoy, as they remind me of how much we actually experience. It's not unlike compiling this newsletter at the end of each week and being reminded of all that has happened in a week -- the previous Monday sometimes feels like months ago. This week, the feature article on findings from education research in 2020 highlighted some of this year's important developments. Similarly, Gallup's workplace lessons are a helpful review.

Also of note this week: the Cartoon Network's PSA is an excellent lesson from a surprising source, the Carnegie report on curriculum-focused PD is a long and well-intended reminder of what actually makes a difference in improving practice, and the Kasparov interview about music could have been filed in Creativity -- it's a reminder that our preconceived notions about topics (like chess and grandmasters) aren't always accurate. In fact, they're probably inaccurate more often than not; Kasparov helps the interviewer realize that a grandmaster can see chess (and life) as more about imagination and creativity than about computation and calculation.

Lastly, 2020 was such a year. Canadian comedian Julia Nolke has produced a quartet of videos on YouTube entitled "explaining the pandemic to my former self." It's comedy, but she also aims for emotional resonance, and it's striking to me, seeing the series in its totality, what a journey this year has been. Enjoy her mix of humor, reflection, and commentary -- and the occasional colorful metaphor.

Happy holidays, everyone!

Peter Nilsson

Vaping Rates Among Teens Level Off, But Are Still High

HealthDay · 12/15/20

“Data from the most recent annual Monitoring the Future survey, conducted by the University of Michigan's Institute for Social Research, show that from 2017 to 2019, the percentage of teens who said they vaped nicotine in the past 12 months doubled: 7.5% to 16.5% among eighth graders; from 15.8% to 30.7%, among 10th graders; and from 18.8% to 35.3% among 12th graders. In 2020, rates held steady at 16.6%, 30.7% and 34.5%, respectively.”
