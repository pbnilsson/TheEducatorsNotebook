Introduction

Some truly extraordinary pieces this week.

How many times do we talk with students about cleaning up at a cafeteria or a dining hall? Usually, we talk about respect for space, and respect for the people who work to support our learning. Now, we have the example of the Japanese World Cup team cleaning up their locker room -- even after winning an extraordinary upset against Germany. (Click on the linked word: "spotless") Could our school teams be this conscientious, even in moments of stunning victories?

Also, find in the feature pieces a provocative piece advocating for the importance of some rote learning in order to develop habits and reflexive learning behaviors. This traditional pedagogical mode seems echoed in the piece later on focusing on oral exams.

More earth-shattering -- so much so that I'll be thinking about this for a long time -- is the Twitter thread linked under STEM. I've for a long time stated that education technology has remained quite crude. Well, that is changing in front of us at this very moment, as artificial intelligence enables human-like responses to complex questions. I resist bold statements like "this will fundamentally change how we teach and assess" -- but this development will fundamentally change important portions of how we teach and assess. See the STEM section for more.

These and more, enjoy!

Peter Nilsson King's Academy Jordan
