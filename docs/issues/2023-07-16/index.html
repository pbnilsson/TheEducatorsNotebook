Happy midsummer, all

It has been two extraordinary weeks -- not just of fireworks and family and friends, but also of developments in A.I. and learning. While the summer is yielding fascinating news and research on teaching and learning, cognitive science, pedagogy and more as always, the A.I. landscape is moving in leaps and bounds. It is almost unbelievable how quickly the field is advancing, and how much both euphoria and doom are circulating in the mediasphere.

For us in education, it is increasingly clear that schools have an enormous opportunity this fall: opportunities for professional growth for teachers, opportunities for customized learning for students, and opportunities for reimagined learning experiences.

It is also clear that every school should be feeling a sense of urgency about preparing for the fall term. This means leadership should be dedicating significant time at opening faculty meetings to share strategies for how the school will proactively navigate the emergence of A.I. Considerations might include:

• Will the school have an organization-wide policy on acceptable use of A.I. -- not only for students but also for faculty and staff?

• What professional learning will ensure all teachers are prepared not only to respond to student use of A.I., but to guide student use of A.I.?

• Has the school identified a lead team of teachers to drive discussion with the rest of the faculty about use of A.I.?

• What orientation will students have to set clear expectations for use of A.I. across their subjects?

• How will organizations like the school newspaper approach the use of A.I. in student writing? What about the literary magazine?

• Are members of the student life and IT offices prepared to handle student use of AI image and video generation?

I generally avoid grandiose statements about technology, but the world is changing, and it will be important to stay abreast of what is unfolding. If this is your domain, start with the second feature in today's issue, in which Ethan Mollick outlines the state of generative A.I. as of this month. Continue with the articles in the "Tech, AI" section(s) which I will be maintaining for the weeks ahead. Last, I will be slowly incrementally adding activities and exercises to Athena for students and teachers to understand what is possible with the new tools we have access to.

But also: the world remains the same. Human interactions remain the essence of what it means to live our fulfilling lives day to day. The first feature article offers a reminder from Psychology Today on what it means to have intellectual humility, to listen closely to others, and to understand that all people -- even those we vehemently disagree with -- believe their perspective to be true to them. How can we -- especially in a world of artificial intelligence -- remember that emotional intelligence is as much if not more important to living not just fully, but also happily.

So much more to also see this week: the politics of school plays, two pieces on the speed of learning, Taylor Swift studies (?!), pronouncing student names, the return of cursive, the importance of the staff room, and the educational value of board games.

All these and more, enjoy!

Peter

People Are More Creative Than Institutions

Social Science Research Network · 06/12/20

“In this study, we empirically assess the contributions of inventors and firms for innovation using a 37-year panel of U.S. patenting activity. We estimate that inventors’ human capital is 5-10 times more important than firm capabilities for explaining the variance in inventor output. We then examine matching between inventors and firms and find highly talented inventors are attracted to firms that (i) have weak firm-specific invention capabilities, and (ii) employ other talented inventors.”
