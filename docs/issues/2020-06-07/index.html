Introduction

Many great resources this week, and much to reflect on.

It has been surreal experiencing these past six months in Jordan. Living on a beautiful campus, in a country with one of the most successful COVID-19 responses in the world (784 cases total), and under a leader actively facilitating global peace and solidarity has been in stark contrast to seeing the country of my nationality and other nations like Hong Kong erupt into large scale protests, all while weathering the global pandemic. The Middle East is often mischaracterized from the outside as uniformly, endlessly mired in violence. But Jordan, at the peaceful heart of the region, has been an island of stability, safety, and beauty. I walk past lavender bushes and olive trees every day, and hear the call to prayer ring across the surrounding farmlands. It has been a privilege to be here.

In this moment of protest surrounding the killing of George Floyd, one must take stock. I have been the beneficiary of a lifetime of privilege. Journeying to another country has helped me recognize it further. Moving from majority to minority, insider to outsider, has made visible my identity, which I didn't have to think about before. No place is free of these racial politics, but we experience them differently, and we grow to understand others' experiences at different times and in different ways. This is a time in which many people are coming to understand the experiences of others much more clearly. May we as teachers further catalyze this growth. Our roles as educators, after all, as the etymology of the verb 'to educate' suggests (Latin: e,ex: out + ducare: to lead -- "to educate" means "to lead out"), is to lead our students out of one place and frame of mind and into another.

Find much this week on the protests emerging around the country and now around the world -- and how we can help build a more just, equitable future.
