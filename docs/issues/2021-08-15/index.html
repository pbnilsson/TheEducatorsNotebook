Introduction

The writing wires were quiet this week, but surfaced a few gems.

The first feature article is a fascinating report by McKinsey about skills and character traits most needed on the job market today. While preparation for the workforce is only one end of education, it is an important one, and this post offers real insight into what skills correlate most with high employment, high income, and high job satisfaction. An excellent and important read.

The second feature post is by a math teacher whose reflections on test-free teaching last year are compelling on two levels: first, they offer a meaningful reflection on how our assessments shape the outcomes of our classes. Second, they offer an excellent example of inquiry based teaching, showing how the teacher's careful methodology for designing and reflecting on class lead to improved practice over time.

Enjoy these and others from this very short issue of the Educator's Notebook!

“I Teach Math, And I Didn’t Give A Single Test Last Year. Here’s What I Learned.”

Blick Bytes · 08/06/21

“Keep scrolling for the long version, but here's the TL;DR: I’m not convinced that students’ recall of math facts or procedures was any better than they would have been with traditional tests; however, I found tremendous evidence indicating that most students understood the concepts and their connections because the assessments gave students agency while requiring them to justify their thinking in connected, authentic contexts. Furthermore, the vast majority of students improved their relationship with the subject because the assessments were more meaningful and less stressful than traditional tests.”
