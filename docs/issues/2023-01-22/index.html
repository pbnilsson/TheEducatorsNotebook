Introduction

Another extraordinary week.

Educators continue to probe the opportunities and limits of this new A.I. technology. Here at King's, we have set aside time in each of our first three faculty meetings to explore both new opportunities for learning (idea generation, writing co-pilot) and new threats to learning (academic integrity, college essays) presented by ChatGPT.

This week's first feature explores the future of writing in this new world, and while the tone of the essay is cynical, it offers some provocative reflections on how ChatGPT might move into the professional world. As a balance to the author's cynicism, I'm reminded that we still teach and practice handwriting in an age of computer keyboards, and we still teach math by hand in an age of calculators. We will still need to teach and practice writing in schools and in our professional lives, but it will happen differently, in different times and in different ways.

The second feature summarizes new research about debate and argument. It suggests that "arguing to learn" rather "arguing to win" increases growth and ultimate success later on. This is a meaningful selection for times of greater polarization.

Later in the issue find two sections on AI (now back in the tech section). Also find several unusual takes in the diversity and inclusion section, a summary of research on the 4-day work week, and predictions from 1923 about what 2023 would be like.

These and more, enjoy!

Peter

On ChatGPT: “AI Is The End Of Writing”

The Spectator · 01/10/23

“It’s at this point that the usual essay on ChatGPT points towards something consoling. Something like ‘Ah, but do not despair, humans will always yada yada’. I’m afraid I am not here to offer any such solace. I’ve done writing of all kinds for several decades, from travel journalism to art journalism to political journalism, from literary fiction to youthful memoirs to notorious-letters-to-No-10 to Fifty Shades porn (a pseudonym) to, lately, religious or domestic thrillers. And I have to say: we are screwed. By which I mean: we, the writers. We’re screwed. Writing is over. That’s it. It’s time to pack away your quill, your biro, and your shiny iPad: the computers will soon be here to do it better.”

How We Debate: Highly Successful People Argue Differently

CNBC · 01/19/23

“Instead of trying to “win” every argument you find yourself in, you could have more success if you look at arguments as opportunities to learn and grow… The setup was simple: Participants had to debate hot-button topics in an online chatroom. One group was instructed to adopt a competitive mentality in order to “win” the argument, while the other group was told to “argue to learn.” An “arguing to learn” mentality rests in viewing contentious conversations as collaborative exchanges that can deepen your understanding of a given topic, rather than battles to be won.”
