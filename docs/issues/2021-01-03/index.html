Introduction

Happy New Year!

An excellent week, with a range of compelling posts.

Our first feature article highlights the dramatic move to digital, and reflects on where people have chosen to engage in their online courses.

The second feature offers insight into the digital reality of adolescents today. Everything is recorded, and the permanence of the digital record both holds people appropriately accountable while also preventing kids from outliving their mistakes. This NYT piece tells the story of one such case in detail.

Elsewhere, enjoy announcements about literary texts entering the public domain, the extraordinary engineering feat of making robots dance, reflections on "the science of reading", and a remarkable body of work deconstructing conspiracy theories.

More generally, may this New Year bring you better rest, a healthy home, and a fresh start to the year.

Enjoy!

Peter Nilsson King's Academy Jordan

FEATURED

2020 Is The Second “Year Of The MOOC”

EdSurge · 12/23/20

“Of all the learners that ever registered on a MOOC platform, one third did so in 2020... Data collected by Class Central show that pre-pandemic, technology-related subjects were the most popular: the ten most-followed courses were all career-focused. Post-pandemic, interest in soft skills and general topics increased. The most popular course during the pandemic turned out to be Yale University’s The Science of Well-Being, with over 2.5 million enrollments in 2020.”
